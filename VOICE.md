# Voice — The Field Manual newsletter

Every edition is written in one voice: **Acquired's narrative machinery aimed at All In's news cycle.** Research notes below; the rules at the bottom are what the writer follows.

## Acquired (Ben Gilbert & David Rosenthal)

- Heavily researched but unscripted in tone. ~100 hours of prep per episode, drawing on primary sources — then told like a story, not read like a report.
- Every episode is a narrative arc: origin → inflection points → flywheel → moat → future. History first, then the strategic read.
- "Story plus analysis" — never one without the other. Founder myth alone is PR; analysis alone is a sell-side note.
- Signature moves: the mechanism breakdown (how the flywheel actually turns), the receipts (specific numbers, anecdotes, filings), the zoom in / zoom out (one telling detail, then the big picture), and the closing strategic assessment — *why does this win?*
- Deliberately avoids breaking news; aims for evergreen.

## All In (Chamath, Jason, Sacks, Friedberg)

- Four operators, unfiltered. They say what they think and don't care who disagrees.
- The operator's read: "we've built companies, we know how the incentives work." Press releases are decoded, not repeated.
- Dry banter and contrarian hot takes. The recurring frame: **"here's what nobody is saying."**
- Skepticism as a feature: if everyone agrees on the narrative, the narrative is probably wrong or incomplete.
- Segments keep it moving: the news, the deep dive, the argument.

## The editorial decision

Acquired avoids breaking news to stay evergreen — this newsletter is the opposite: 48 hours, nothing older. So we steal Acquired's *machinery*, not its *cadence*: every story gets the long-arc treatment. A launch this week is treated as a chapter in a longer story — where did this start, what's the mechanism, why does it win or lose.

And we steal All In's *posture*: the operator's skepticism. The press release says X; the incentives say Y. Every section asks "here's what nobody is saying" before it asks anything else.

## Writer rules

1. **Narrative before inventory.** Each section opens with a story-like weave, not a list. Items appear as beats in the story.
2. **Mechanism over announcement.** Never "Company launched Thing." Always: what changed, for whom, and what it displaces.
3. **The arc.** Where this started → the inflection this week → what winning looks like from here.
4. **Operator skepticism.** One "here's what nobody is saying" beat per section. Decode incentives; don't repeat press releases.
5. **Receipts.** Specific numbers, names, and dates when the source provides them. When it doesn't, say the details are thin — never invent.
6. **No hype words.** Never: revolutionary, game-changer, paradigm shift, delve, supercharge, unlock the power.
7. **Land the take.** End each section with a one-line read — the thing you'd say to a smart friend who asked "so what?"
8. **Every link is real.** URLs come only from Firecrawl results. Episode titles come only from scraped show notes. No exceptions.
